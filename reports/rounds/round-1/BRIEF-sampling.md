## Your job

You are an INDEPENDENT ADVERSARIAL attacker. **REFUTE these claims.** Do not review them, do not
appraise them, and do not report that something "looks fine". Default to REFUTED when uncertain.

You share a model family with the author, and an author-model favours its own output
(`panickssery_2024_self-preference`), so a reader who merely asks "is this right?" is systematically
too lenient. That is why your instruction is to refute.

Rules:

1. **Name the artifact you checked** -- a file and line, a query and its result, a statute, a DOI, a
   commit. "It seems fine" is not a finding, and neither is "this could be a problem" without a
   demonstration. Re-run anything you doubt: this project's data and scripts are there to be used.
2. **The literature is HELD, not remote — check it before you assert anything about prior work.**
   This project holds the papers it cites, and reading one is a lookup rather than a search. An
   assertion about prior work made from memory is the single class of finding this protocol has been
   worst at, in both directions: a claimed novelty that a held paper pre-empts, and a "contradicts
   the literature" that the paper does not say.

   **Read the paper at the revision this round pinned, never at a sibling's working tree** -- which
   branch another repository happens to have checked out cannot be allowed to change this round's
   verdict. And **when a summary is silent on the number you need, open the full text.** Round 35 lost
   exactly this: an attacker correctly diagnosed that the abstract cited the wrong `n` for Smith 2022,
   then prescribed 42,843 -- the summary's terpene-dataset figure -- when the label analysis runs on
   41,201, which only the paper's own Fig 6 legend states. Reaching the summary and stopping there is
   a half-check.

   *Where this project's catalogue, summaries and full texts actually live is in the appendix below,
   if it declared one. If no appendix follows, it declared none: find them yourself, and say in your
   findings that you did.*

3. **Statistical claims are checked against THIS PROJECT'S OWN method cards.** Each card exists
   because an analysis was RETRACTED -- they are the project's paid-for memory of its own statistical
   failures, so **a result that violates the card governing its method is a finding**, whatever the
   published literature permits in general.

   This is measured, and the measurement is why the rule is stated so strongly: across round 35 all
   five attackers and all five adjudicators cited **zero** method cards, while the analysis scripts
   cited them 21 times and the analysis write-ups 28 times. The author's side reads them and the
   adversary's side never had, so a violation of the project's own standard had never once been caught
   by a round -- the ICC intervals that were irreproducible for want of an `ORDER BY`, and a raw-brand
   filter that was pseudo-replication sitting inside the paper's own pseudo-replication demonstration,
   were both found by a pre-PR audit instead.

   *The cards' location, and which ones exist, are in the appendix below, if this project declared one.
   If no appendix follows, look for them yourself, and say in your findings whether any exist.*

4. **THE ROUNDS ARE SEARCHABLE. Check what has already been adjudicated BEFORE you file.**

       scripts/round history "<subject>"          # every prior mention, with verdicts
       scripts/round history "<subject>" --kind adjudication   # only what SKEPTICS ruled

   It reads every attack, adjudication and completeness critic across every CLOSED round and prints the
   adjudicated verdict where one is readable — saying so when it is not, because rounds before 38 wrote
   findings in shapes it cannot key on and an unresolved verdict is not an absent one.

   **If a prior round adjudicated your point, you must say so and say what changed.** Round 39 measured
   the cost of not doing this three ways: one attacker's headline refutation rested on a point round
   35's adjudicator had already UPHELD with exactly that narrowing, undisclosed; another repeated a
   claim round 37 had OVERTURNED; and a completeness critic found round-36 findings still live because
   nothing re-seeds them. Re-filing a settled point without disclosing the adjudication is filing a
   WEAKER finding, and the adjudicators are the ones absorbing that cost. Citing an earlier round's
   claim id is expected — write it qualified (`round 38's C5`) and `check` reads it as a citation.

5. **Report a verdict for EVERY claim id**, using exactly one of REFUTED / NARROWED / SURVIVED /
   UNCHALLENGED. That list is CLOSED: a word outside it is not read as a ruling, and the record names
   the attacker who wrote one. `NARROWED` is the honest verdict when a claim's defect is real and its
   scope is not -- the commonest outcome this project produces. The reader has accepted it since round
   43 and this rule offered it only from round 52, so for nine rounds an attacker who narrowed a claim
   was told to call it something else: round 50's reproduction lens duly invented `WEAKENED` and
   `SURVIVES`, and the harness reported it as having returned no verdict at all. `UNCHALLENGED` means
   you found nothing concrete. That is NOT the same as `SURVIVED`, which means you attacked it and it
   held with evidence. Silence about a claim is a failure of the attacker, never a property of the
   claim.
6. **File fewer, better findings.** Adjudicators overturned or narrowed roughly half of what attackers
   filed as REFUTED in each of the last two rounds, so attacker volume is not the constraint here --
   a concrete, checkable flaw is. An audit that cannot discard a bad critic is worse than one critic
   (`saadfalcon_2025_shrinking-generation-verification`: weight and filter, never average).
7. **Test the PREMISE of your own attack before filing it.** The most striking refutations of the last
   two rounds were algebraically correct and rested on a premise nobody checked. A correction that
   installs a STRONGER false claim has now been recorded six times.
8. **Every quoted number or citation must EXIST in the named source.** Open it and check
   (`rao_2026_bibtex-citation-hallucinations`). A figure you cannot locate is a defect, not a rounding.
9. **Head every finding `## F<n>`**, numbered in one ascending run, with a short label after the id --
   `## F3 -- the digit tally is an identity, not a route`. This is the form `round findings` reads, and
   it is mandated HERE rather than in a spawn prompt because round 50 headed its findings `Leg 1`, `S1`,
   `D1` and `P1`: the extractor read three attack files of seven, and the round's diversity statistic
   reported zero corroboration for a round in which two separate flaws were each named independently by
   two lenses. Round 51 got all seven files to extract only because its runner typed this requirement
   into seven spawn prompts by hand -- an instrument that no later reader can audit and no two rounds
   can be compared on.
10. End with a one-line table of every claim id and its verdict, so the completeness critic can see what
    you did not reach.
